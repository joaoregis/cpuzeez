Know what's in your PC and what holds it back.

CPU ZeeZ reads your processor, memory, mainboard, graphics and drives, watches every sensor, shows FPS over your games and tells you what limits them. Free and open source, for Windows, Linux and macOS. Nothing leaves your computer.

One app instead of five

The details a hardware report gives you, a monitor that runs next to your games, and the tools to find out why a game stutters.

Everything CPU-Z reports

Processor and topology, caches, mainboard and BIOS, memory timings and SPD from DDR to DDR5, graphics and PCI. Reports in TXT, HTML and JSON, and the same command-line flags.

A hardware monitor

Every sensor with minimum, maximum and average, live charts and CSV logging. Throttling detection, tray icons per metric and small always-on-top monitors.

FPS over your games

ZeeZ Live draws an overlay in its own window, so nothing is injected into the game. FPS, frame time, 1% and 0.1% lows and GPU Busy, with captures, reports and comparisons.

What limits your frame rate

The bottleneck detector measures, then says GPU at its limit, CPU at its limit (core 4), a frame cap, throttling, full video memory or another program, with the numbers behind it.

A Task Manager on every system

Processes with network, power and GPU use, performance graphs, app history, startup items, users and services. Anything risky asks first.

Benchmarks and a stability test

CPU, memory and cache, GPU, storage and core-to-core latency, each with history and comparison. The stability test stops itself at temperature limits.

See it first

Windows 11 on a Ryzen 9 7900X with an RTX 4060. Serial numbers, MACs and IPs are masked.

Private by design

CPU ZeeZ works entirely on your computer.

  • No telemetry and no account. The app sends nothing about you or your PC anywhere.
  • Off until you allow it: the update check (it asks GitHub for the latest release), app history and recording.
  • Rights per action. Something that needs administrator or root asks for it for that one action, and every change is written to a local log.
  • Open source. Every line is on GitHub under MIT or Apache-2.0, and every release is built by GitHub Actions from that code.
